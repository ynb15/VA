# Seeking Discomfort since 1998

Three days into my senior year, I walked away from a $150,000 soccer scholarship
in New York City.

It was peak-Covid. The world was closed. I had a thousand dollars, a one-way
ticket and no plan.

My mom is still mad at you boys for that one.

Because it wasn't courage, and I want to be honest about that. It was four guys
on YouTube and a feeling I'd stopped being able to argue with.

I'd found you from a dorm room in New York, on the nights when the promising
future everyone kept congratulating me on felt like it belonged to somebody
else. Strangers talked into skydiving. Blindfolded drops into countries nobody
picked. And then Will Smith's 50th birthday: a bungee jump out of a helicopter
over the Grand Canyon.

Four people living their lives in a way I had never seen anybody do it.

The more I watched, the thinner my own dream looked. Then, one restless night in
that dorm bed, I decided it was time to take the leap.

## The rest is history

I ended up travelling five continents and living in New York City, Medellín,
Berlin, Bali and Cape Town, building my own copywriting agency along the way.
Five years down the road, my vision board had become my everyday life, and I
have never once regretted that leap of faith.

But at some point, things started to shift.

At the pinnacle I was running the marketing for Sir Richard Branson, promoting
his retreats on his private island in the Caribbean. I was getting invited by
Google and offered a six-figure consulting gig in Switzerland at my dream
company.

Once again, supposed to be living the dream. Once again, something wasn't
sitting right.

It took me a while to work out that this feeling has never really been about
dissatisfaction. It's about reinvention.

Nothing stays alive by staying the same. Thomas has said as much about Yes
Theory's own content, how it had to keep changing to stay real. Seek Discomfort
isn't a one-time jump out of a life you hate. It's the thing you have to do
again and again - even when it means letting go of something you once dreamed
of.

## Burning the second life

So I finally started something I'd been thinking about for years. I began
sharing content on YouTube, documented a 30-day ice bath challenge, vlogged my
life in Cape Town and committed to living "the last year of my life".

And the more I exposed myself to others, the harder it became to keep lying to
myself.

Still, it took my first AfrikaBurn and a rite of passage to make me jump again.

Coming out of the desert, I finally made the decisions I had been avoiding. I
let go of my business. I let go of the old version of myself. Dropped the mask
and burned it with everything else that went up in flames out there. Three days
post-Burn, The Wolf of Copywriting was gone.

Trying to figure out what was supposed to come next in my book of life, I did
what any reasonable Seeker would do: I took a sabbatical, flew to India and
spent some time in an ashram - to maybe find God somewhere along the way and ask
Him what to do with the next chapter.

Two months knee-deep in the rather radical philosophy of Tantra, I didn't manage
to find Him. But at least the calling to start a podcast.

To symbolise the leap, I made sure to kick my own ass out of a plane at 10,000
feet. That gave me the final courage - or rather the madness - to burn the
bridges, leave the safe, successful career for good, fly across the globe on my
last savings and bet everything on one mission:

To build one of the most impactful podcasts the world has ever listened to.

I named it after Frankl's book, Man's Search for Meaning. It tells the
unfiltered stories of people who've walked through the fire and came out
different.

Thanks to this mad obsession of mine and an absurd number of cold emails - ten,
then twenty, then fifty, and then three hundred - the most extraordinary people
somehow started saying yes:

- ex-Bill Clinton attorney Ken Stern
- the world's leading quantum scientist and OpenAI insider Prof. Scott Aaronson
- UFC fighter Kyle Kingsbury
- Hollywood star turned men's therapist David Sutcliffe
- NFL star Eben Britton
- the world's leading DMT and consciousness researcher Prof. Rick Strassman
- $100M EdTech founder and Ironman athlete Rob Paddock, who went on to become my
  mentor

And then I somehow got invited to Burning Man HQ in San Francisco to record a
pod with their CEO.

## The one I didn't choose

Then a motorcycle I was riding came off a mountain road in Colorado at 90 km/h.

Every other discomfort in this essay I walked into on purpose. The dropout. The
one-way ticket. The open plane door. The business I buried myself. This one
walked into me, and it is by far the hardest challenge I have ever been handed.

And it's the same two words getting me through it. The accident took the
momentum I'd spent a year building and vaporised it. Bones heal on a schedule.
Momentum doesn't. Between the rehab and the medical bills, falling into a hole
would be the easiest thing I've ever done - and nobody would blame me for it.
Seek Discomfort is the reason I'm not in it.

I'm writing this from rehab in Cape Town. Still building.

## And now they're closing it

Eight years. Close to half a million orders. Around 940,000 pieces in 155
countries. No more drops coming.

They said something in that goodbye video I haven't been able to put down: every
piece still out there has the potential to spark a connection between two
complete strangers. That was never about clothing. It was a way for people who
had made the same decision to recognise each other across a room.

I've been carrying those two words since a dorm room in New York. They cost me a
scholarship, a career and a company - and handed me a life I would choose again
every single time.

So I sent them a pitch for what comes next.

Whatever they do with it, the mantra isn't going anywhere. It's the thing that
got me off a soccer field and onto a plane, out of an agency and into a desert,
and it's the thing getting me out of bed in a rehab centre in Cape Town.

Seeking discomfort since 1998.
