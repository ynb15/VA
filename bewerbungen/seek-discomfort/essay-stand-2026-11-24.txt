Essay: Seeking Discomfort since 1998

“Life begins at the end of your comfort zone.” 
Neale D. Walsch 

These two words have shaped my life more than any other. 

It all started when I first discovered YesTheory back when I was in college. 

A 150k soccer scholarship in New York City. A promising future. I was supposed to be “living the dream”. But something just wasn’t sitting right… 

Then one day I stumbled upon four lads on YouTube: approaching strangers, talking them into skydiving, getting dropped blindfolded in a random country with no money in their pockets. Somehow making Will Smith bungee jump out of a helicopter over the Grand Canyon for his 50th birthday. 

→ Will Smith meme?! 

Four guys actually living their dreams, in a way I had never seen anybody do it.

The more I watched their content, the thinner my own "dream" looked. Then, one restless night in my college dorm bed in NYC, I decided it was time for me to take the leap… 

Three days into my senior year, I dropped out, waved goodbye to the 150k, packed my bag and went on a trip around the world - during peak-Covid, with nothing but 1.000 bucks in my bank account and absolutely no plan. 

(Btw my mom’s still mad at you for that, boys)

The rest is history. 

I ended up traveling 5 continents and I’ve lived in New York City, Medellin, Berlin, Bali and Cape Town, while building my own copywriting agency. 5 years down the road, my vision board had become my everyday life and I never regretted having made that leap of faith. 



But at some point, things started to shift. At the pinnacle, I was running the marketing for Sir Richard Branson, getting invited by Google and offered a 6-figure consulting gig in Switzerland at my dream company. Once again, supposed to be living the dream. But once again, something wasn’t sitting right. 

It took me a while to work out that this feeling has never really been about dissatisfaction - but about reinvention. 

Nothing stays alive by staying the same. Thomas has said as much about YesTheory's own content, how it had to keep changing to stay real. Seek Discomfort isn't a one-time jump out of a life you hate. 

It's the thing you have to do again and again, even if it means letting go of something you once dreamed of.

So I finally started something I’d been thinking about for years: I began sharing content on YouTube, documented a 30-day ice bath challenge, vlogged my life in Cape Town and committed myself to live “the last year of my life”. 

And the more I exposed myself to others, the harder it became to keep lying to myself… 

Yet, it took my first AfrikaBurn and a rite of passage to make me jump again: 

 

Coming out of the desert, I finally made the decisions I had been avoiding. I let go of my business. I let go of the old version of myself. Dropped the mask. Burned it with everything else that went up in flames out there. Three days post-Burn, The Wolf of Copywriting was gone. 

Trying to figure out what was supposed to come next in my book of life, I did what any reasonable Seeker would do: I took a sabbatical, flew to India to spend some time in an ashram - and maybe find God somewhere along the way and ask him what to do with the next chapter… 

Two months knee-deep into the rather radical philosophy of Tantra, I unfortunately didn’t manage to find Him - but at least the calling to start a podcast. 

To symbolize this leap of faith, I made sure to kick my ass out of a plane and skydive from 10.000 feet. That gave the final courage (or rather madness?) to burn the bridges, leave my “safe, successful career” for good, fly across the globe on my last savings and bet everything on this one mission:

To build one of the most impactful podcasts the world has ever listened to.

I named it after Frankl’s book Man's Search for Meaning - and it tells the unfiltered stories of people who've walked through the fire and came out different.
Thanks to this mad obsession of mine and an absurd amount of cold emails (ten, then, twenties, and then fifty - and then three hundred), the most extraordinary people somehow started saying yes…
