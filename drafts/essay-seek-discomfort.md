# Seeking Discomfort since 1998

*College dropout. One-way ticket. Still going.*

> "Life begins at the end of your comfort zone."
> — Neale Donald Walsch

Two words have shaped my life more than any others.

It all started back when I was in college. A 150k soccer scholarship in New York
City. A promising future. I was supposed to be "living the dream" — but something
just wasn't sitting right.

Then one day I stumbled upon four lads on YouTube. Approaching strangers. Talking
them into skydiving. Getting dropped blindfolded in a country they hadn't picked,
with no money in their pockets. Will Smith's 50th birthday turning into a bungee
jump out of a helicopter over the Grand Canyon. Four guys actually living their
dreams, in a way I had never seen anybody do it.

The more I watched their content, the thinner my own "dream" looked.

Then I took the leap.

I dropped out of college in my senior year, waved goodbye to the 150k, packed my
bags and went on a trip around the world — during peak-Covid, with nothing but
1,000 bucks in my bank account and absolutely no plan.

(Btw my mom's still mad at you for that, boys)

The rest is history.

I ended up traveling five continents and living in New York City, Medellín,
Berlin, Bali and Cape Town, while building my own copywriting agency from zero.
At the pinnacle I was running the marketing for Sir Richard Branson, promoting
his retreats on his private island in the Caribbean, getting invited by Google
and offered a $100k consulting gig in Switzerland.

Five years down the road, I had everything I had written on the list that got me
on that plane in the first place. And I've never regretted having made that leap
of faith.

But at some point, this started to shift. Once again, supposed to be living the
dream. Once again, something wasn't sitting right.

It took me a while to work out that this feeling has never really been about
dissatisfaction. It's about reinvention. Nothing stays alive by staying the same —
Thomas has said as much about Yes Theory's own content, how it had to keep
changing to stay honest. Seeking discomfort isn't a one-time jump out of a life
you hate. It's the thing you have to do again, from inside a life you actually
built.

So I finally started something I'd been thinking about for years. I began sharing
content on YouTube, documented a 30-day ice bath challenge, vlogged my life in
Cape Town — and committed myself to live "the last year of my life". But the more
I exposed myself to others, the harder it became to keep lying to myself.

It took my first AfrikaBurn and a rite of passage to make me jump again.

Coming out of the desert, I finally made the decisions I had been avoiding. I let
go of my business. I let go of the old version of myself. Dropped the mask and
burned it with everything else that went up in flames out there. Three days
post-Burn I shut the company down. The Wolf of Copywriting was gone for good.

Trying to figure out what was supposed to come next in my book of life, I did what
any reasonable Seeker would do: I flew to India to spend some time in an ashram —
and maybe find God somewhere along the way and ask Him what to do with the next
chapter.

Two months knee-deep in the rather radical philosophy of Tantra, I unfortunately
didn't manage to find Him. But at least the calling to start a podcast.

To symbolize this leap of faith, I made sure to kick my ass out of a plane and
skydive from 10,000 feet. That gave me the final courage (or rather madness?) to
burn the bridges, leave my "safe, successful career" for good, fly across the
globe on my last savings and bet everything on this one mission:

To build one of the most impactful podcasts the world has ever listened to.

I named it after Frankl's book, Man's Search for Meaning, and it tells the
unfiltered stories of people who've walked through the fire and came out
different.

Thanks to this mad obsession of mine and an absurd amount of cold emails — ten,
then twenty, then fifty, and then three hundred — the most extraordinary people
somehow started saying yes:

- Burning Man CEO Marian Goodell
- Ex-Bill Clinton lawyer Ken Stern
- Quantum scientist and OpenAI insider Prof. Scott Aaronson
- The world's leading DMT researcher Rick Strassman
- NFL star Eben Britton
- UFC fighter Kyle Kingsbury
- Hollywood star turned men's therapist David Sutcliffe
- EdTech-millionaire, GetSmarter founder and Ironman athlete Rob Paddock, who went
  on to become my mentor

I even got invited to the Burning Man HQ in SF to record a pod with their CEO.

And then, in September, a motorcycle I was riding on came off a mountain road in
Colorado at 90 km/h.

I'm writing this from rehab in Cape Town.

This is the one I didn't choose. Every other discomfort in this essay I walked
into on purpose — the dropout, the one-way ticket, the open plane door, the
business I killed myself. This one walked into me, and it's by far the hardest
challenge I've ever been handed.

And it's the same two words getting me through it. Rehab is a fork in the road
every single morning: the comfortable version of the day, or the one that costs
something and gives something back. I'm still building MSFM from here.

So when I heard that the guys who infected me with the Seeker-virus in the first
place are looking for someone to carry the brand into its next chapter, it felt
like being called again.

Because this is the exact intersection I've been standing in for seven years:
community, marketing, leadership. I've run retreats, masterminds and workations
across Europe, Asia and Africa, inside communities like ManKindProject, Art of
Mondays and Founders Running Club. I've built brands from Germany's leading
psychedelic therapy company to my own six-figure agency from zero. And I only
ever record the podcast in person, on five continents so far.

Marketing strategist, podcast host, community architect. 3-in-1. All-in for Seek
Discomfort.

You built the audience. I'll build the movement.
