What if the man OpenAI hired to help make AI safe spent two years being asked one question he still can't answer – what would it mean, mathematically, for a machine to love humanity?

Scott Aaronson is one of the world's leading quantum computing theorists. He teaches computer science at UT Austin, where he founded the Quantum Information Center, and he has spent his career on one question: what can computers ever do, and what can they never do? Then in 2022 Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike asked him to take a leave and work on AI safety at OpenAI – and while he was there, he invented the first scheme for watermarking the output of a large language model.

We start with quantum computers and end up somewhere I hadn't planned: God, the Book of Job, October 7th, and what he tells his own kids about a world where machines may soon do everything better than we can. In between he says the thing almost no one in his field will say out loud – that the trouble with AI isn't that it doesn't work…

In this episode we explore:

• Ilya Sutskever's question: what would it mean, mathematically, for an AI to love humanity?
• The ChatGPT watermark he invented in 2022 – and why OpenAI never shipped it
• The AI models that falsified an Erdős conjecture and moved the Riemann hypothesis
• AI 2027, human extinction, and how orangutans fare in a human world
• Arthur C. Clarke: maybe our role is not to worship God, but to create him

An unfiltered, deeply human conversation about intelligence, God, extinction and what will be left for us to do.
